Hi Gaveriel,

I’ve been thinking about how to say this without making it sound too complicated, so I think the best way is simply to be honest with you.

I want to know you more. Not just the things you casually tell me, or the little details I can pick up from our conversations. I want to know the person behind all of those things—what makes you happy, what makes you feel safe, what you’re passionate about, what scares you, what you dream about, and even the parts of yourself you don’t easily show to other people.

Somewhere along the way, I realised that I don’t want to get to know you half-heartedly. I want to be intentional about you. I don’t think I can genuinely discover what this connection could become if I’m dividing my attention, time, and emotions between different people. If I’m choosing to know you, I want to give you a fair and sincere version of me—the one that is actually present.

Hindi naman ibig sabihin na kailangan nating madaliin kung ano man ’to. I’m not asking you to immediately put a label on us. I’m not asking you to promise me something you’re not ready to promise. I just want us to give whatever is growing between us a genuine chance.

And there’s something else I’ve been wanting to tell you.

Gaveriel, puwede ba kitang ligawan?

Nakakakaba siyang sabihin because, truthfully, this will be my first time courting someone. I don’t have experience to rely on. I don’t have some perfect formula telling me what I’m supposed to say, when I’m supposed to give you flowers, how often I should surprise you, or what the “correct” way of courting someone is.

Maybe I’ll be awkward sometimes. Maybe I’ll overthink whether I’m doing things right. Maybe there will be moments when I genuinely won’t know what I’m doing. But if you allow me, I want to learn—not just how to court someone, but how to court you.

Let me take my time learning you, Gaveriel, so that if one day this grows into love, I can love you not only in the way I know how to love, but also in the way your heart wants to be loved.

I want to learn what makes you feel appreciated, what kind of reassurance comforts you, what makes you smile after an exhausting day, when you need someone beside you, and when you simply need space. I want to remember the little things—even something as simple as how much you love matcha.

And yes, even the MedTech things I probably won’t understand immediately. You can tell me about your subjects, laboratory work, exams, duties, or some ridiculously complicated medical term, and even if I have to ask you what it means five times, I’ll listen.

Maybe that’s the Data Analyst in me. I like understanding things. But with you, I don’t want to analyse you. I want to understand you.

I also want you to know the real version of me. Not just Ches when I’m confident, playful, clingy, or maharot. There are parts of myself that I’m still learning to accept too.

I have a condition that has caused changes in my body and made me gain weight without me consciously choosing it. After the operation I had last year, my colon processes food more slowly than it normally should. Sometimes, that makes loving my own body difficult.

I know I have imperfections, and I know my body may continue to change. Someday, I want someone who can look beyond all of that—someone who doesn’t measure my worth by my weight or by how my body looks, but someone who can love the person living inside it.

And if we ever reach the point where what we have becomes love, I hope I can feel safe knowing that you love me—not a particular version of my body that I have to maintain just to remain worthy of your affection.

In return, I want to give you that same kind of love. I don’t expect you to be perfect. I don’t want some carefully curated version of you. I want to learn your imperfections too—your bad days, your insecurities, your weird habits, your stressful MedTech days, your quiet days, your happy days, and everything in between.

Because courting you, at least for me, isn’t about convincing you that I’m perfect for you. It’s about giving you the opportunity to know me sincerely enough to decide whether I’m someone you’d want beside you.

So I don’t want to make promises that sound beautiful now but become difficult to keep later. Instead, I can promise you effort, consistency, patience, communication, respect, and the willingness to keep learning.

I’ll probably make mistakes because this is my first time. But I hope you’ll tell me when I do. Teach me how your heart wants to be handled, and I’ll do my best to handle it gently.

And if there comes a day when you choose me too, I hope we never stop learning each other. Because people change. Dreams change. Bodies change. Life changes. And I think loving someone means continuously meeting every new version of them and saying, “Okay. Let me learn you again.”

So, Gaveriel, puwede ba kitang ligawan slowly? Puwede ba kitang alagaan without making you feel pressured? Puwede ba akong maging someone you can run to after a tiring day? Puwede ba kitang yayain for matcha dates and listen while you tell me about things you learned in MedTech that I probably won’t understand at first?

Puwede ba kitang kilalanin hanggang sa maintindihan ko hindi lang kung ano ang nagpapasaya sa’yo, kundi pati kung paano mo gustong mahalin?

And maybe, if life is kind to us, puwede bang dumating yung araw na hindi ko na kailangang itanong kung may lugar ba ako sa puso mo, because you chose to make one for me?

For now, I’m not asking for forever. I’m simply asking for permission to begin—to show you my intentions instead of only telling you about them, to learn you, to choose you intentionally, and to see where two people who are genuinely trying might eventually find themselves.

If your answer is yes, please send me “el gato” on Messenger. 🐱🤍

If I receive “el gato” from you on Messenger, I’ll know what your answer means. I’ll know that you’re giving me the chance to pursue you—not to rush you, not to immediately call you mine, but to slowly earn a place in your heart.

And knowing myself, I’ll probably stare at that message for a few seconds, smile like an idiot, screenshot it, and remember the exact moment you gave me my first chance to genuinely court someone. HAHAHA.

From that moment, I don’t want to suddenly overwhelm you with grand gestures or pretend that I already know everything about romance. I want to start simply—with conversations that last a little longer, remembering how you like your matcha, checking on you after an exhausting MedTech day, listening when you want to tell me about your studies, and slowly showing you that when I told you I wanted to be intentional about you, I meant it.

Because receiving that “el gato” wouldn’t mean I’ve already won your heart. It would mean you’ve given me permission to try to earn it. And I think that’s even more beautiful.

But Gaveriel, please only send it if it’s something you genuinely want. I don’t want your yes because this letter made you feel guilty, pressured, or obligated to give me a chance. I want your yes only if it’s a yes your heart genuinely wants to give.

And if your answer is not yet, or even if it’s no, tell me honestly. I’ll respect you. Because even before becoming your manliligaw, I want one of the first ways I care for you to be respecting your heart and your decisions.

But if one day I open Messenger and see “el gato” from you, I think that will become one of my favourite notifications I’ve ever received.

This may be my first time courting someone, but I’m really glad the person who made me brave enough to try is you.

May God guide whatever this is becoming and allow it to grow only if it is something good, sincere, and meant for both of us.

With sincerity, a little kaba, and a lot of affection,

Yours truly, Ches

P.S. If I receive “el gato” from you on Messenger, please be patient with your first-time manliligaw. I might not know everything yet, but if you’ll let me, I’ll learn—not just how to court someone, but how to court you. 🤍
